Mass Google Indexing: How to Index 500+ Pages in 24 Hours

Your store has 800 products, but only 100 are visible in Google? A blog with 200 articles, but traffic only goes to 30? This is an indexing problem — and it’s solved not by waiting, but by the Google Indexing API. We analyze why Google doesn't index all pages and how to fix it systematically.

Situation: 500 Pages and None in Google

You spent months creating content. A store with 800 products, a blog with 200 articles, or a corporate site with dozens of landing pages — and Google only has 50-100 of them. The rest seem not to exist.

This is not rare. Ahrefs research shows that over 90% of pages on the Internet receive zero organic traffic from Google. One of the main reasons is that they simply aren't indexed.

The good news: mass indexing can be solved systematically, rather than waiting for years.

Why Doesn't Google Index All Pages

Googlebot works within a crawl budget — a limit on the number of pages it's willing to crawl per unit of time. For new or low-authority sites, this limit is minimal.

When you have 1000 pages and Googlebot is ready to visit 100, it chooses the most important ones based on its criteria. The remaining 900 wait in line. For weeks. For months.

  • New site under 6 months old — minimal crawl budget
  • Site without backlinks — Google considers it unimportant
  • Pages without internal links — hard to find at all
  • Duplicates and thin content — reduce overall crawl budget
⚠ What Waiting Won't Solve

If the problem is crawl budget or the lack of links to pages, adding new content won't improve the situation. A systematic approach to submitting URLs for reindexing is needed.

Methods That Don't Scale

Search Console "Request Indexing"

The URL inspection tool in Search Console allows you to submit one URL for indexing. Max 10–15 URLs per day. If you have 500 pages, it will take over a month of manual work.

Ping Google Sitemap

A request to https://www.google.com/ping?sitemap=URL tells Google that the sitemap has updated. Google will process it... when it wants to. Faster than without a ping, but still days or weeks.

Manual Link Building

Effective for long-term SEO, but doesn't solve the mass indexing problem right now.

The Only Solution for Mass Indexing: Google Indexing API

Google Indexing API is Google's official tool for directly notifying about new or updated URLs. Unlike a sitemap ping, it's not a "hint" but essentially a command for Googlebot to visit a specific page.

The result: most URLs are indexed within 24–48 hours after the request is submitted.

📊 Real Statistics

According to our clients: after submission via Indexing API, 75–90% of URLs appear in Google within a day. Without the API, the average indexing time for a new page is 14–60 days.

Preparing the Sitemap for Mass Indexing

Before sending, it's important to clean the sitemap of "junk" URLs — they will waste the API limit to no avail:

  • Remove URLs with the noindex tag — Google will ignore them anyway
  • Remove pagination pages if they are closed by canonical
  • Remove empty categories and tags without content
  • Do not include URLs with filter parameters (?color=red)
  • Leave only pages with unique quality content

What to Do After Mass Indexing

Indexing is just the first step. For pages to start ranking:

  • Check Search Console in 48–72 hours — all URLs should be in the "URL is on Google" status
  • Add internal links to new pages from popular sections
  • For important pages — get at least a few external links
  • Set up automatic submission of new publications via IndexFast — so you don't get into this situation again

Conclusion

Mass indexing is solved by the Google Indexing API — and only by it. Manual submission via Search Console doesn't scale. Waiting for Googlebot can take months.
