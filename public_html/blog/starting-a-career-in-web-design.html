Career in Web Design: How to Start from Scratch and Get First Orders

Web design is one of the highest-paid and creative professions of the digital age. If you love beautiful interfaces, are attentive to details, and want to work for yourself, this guide is for you.

What is Web Design and who can do it?

Web design isn't just about "making things pretty." It is a complex discipline combining visual design, User Experience (UX), typography, composition, and basic HTML/CSS knowledge.

The profession doesn't require formal education. Many successful designers started with online courses, self-study, and practice. The main thing is a portfolio and the ability to present your solutions.

5 Skills Every Web Designer Needs

1. Figma or other design tools

Figma is currently the industry standard. Learn components, auto-layouts, text styles, and plugins.

2. HTML/CSS Basics

You don't have to be a full-fledged developer, but understanding how a design is coded is mandatory. This allows for better communication with the frontend team.

3. UX Research

Knowing how to hypothesize, create personas, build information architecture, and run A/B tests distinguishes a junior from an experienced specialist.

4. Typography and Composition

Fonts, grids, contrast, visual hierarchy—all this affects the convenience of content consumption.

5. Client Communication

The ability to listen, ask the right questions, and present your work is your key currency in freelancing.

💡 Tip

Create a pet project (a site for a fictional client) and go through the full cycle: from brief to finished layout.

How to create a portfolio when you have zero experience

Without a portfolio, you won't get any orders. But how to fill it if there are no real projects yet?

  1. Redesign a famous brand — take a company's site you like and redesign it.
  2. Concept projects — create a design for an app that doesn't exist yet.
  3. Participate in challenges — Daily UI, Figma Community Challenges.
  4. Help non-profit projects — make a site for a local community or volunteer organization for free.

Where to look for your first orders

When your portfolio is ready (3-5 high-quality works), it's time to look for clients:

  • Freelance platforms: Upwork, Fiverr. You need to start with a low price for ratings.
  • Telegram communities: Freelance groups and chats.
  • Direct outreach: write to 20 small and medium businesses you like.
  • Networking: tell friends and relatives on social networks that you are looking for clients.

How to set prices at the initial stage

Beginners often make two mistakes: underpricing or overpricing. Recommendation:

  • Research competitors' prices with 0-1 year of experience.
  • Never work without prepayment (30-50%).
  • For the first projects, take 20-30% below the market, but not below $300 per landing page.
  • After each project, increase prices by 15-20%.

Tools that save time

A professional differs from an amateur not only in skills but also in tools. Here is the minimum set:

  • Figma — design
  • Notion — project management and client briefing
  • Google Workspace — communication
  • IndexFast — automatic indexing of client sites in Google. If you make sites for clients, offer indexing as an extra service.

Common mistakes of beginners

  • Not investing in yourself. If you don't invest in training and tools, your level will stagnate.
  • Taking everything. At the beginning, choose a niche and dive deep into it.
  • Ignoring soft skills. The ability to dialogue and argue is 50% of success.
  • Not writing a contract. Always fix the conditions: deadlines, price, edits, ownership.

How to move from freelance to your own studio

After 1-2 years of stable orders, you can think about scaling:

  1. Hiring an assistant. First outsourced, then a full-time designer.
  2. Fixed packages. Instead of hourly pay, packages like "landing + support".
  3. Partnerships with developers. Together you offer a full cycle.
  4. Your own brand. A blog on TG or YouTube, cases on Dribbble/Behance.
