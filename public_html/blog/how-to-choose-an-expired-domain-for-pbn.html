How to choose an expired domain for PBN: step-by-step guide 2026

Building a PBN (Private Blog Network) is one of the most powerful ways to build link mass. However, the success of the entire network depends on the quality of the expired domains chosen. We break down step by step how to find the perfect drop and not waste your budget on spammed trash.

What is an expired domain and why is it needed for a PBN

An expired domain (drop domain) is a domain name that previously belonged to another owner but was not renewed in time and became available for registration again.

The main value of an expired domain lies in its link profile (backlinks) and history. Instead of spending years building a new site from scratch, you buy a domain that is already linked to by trusted resources, Wikipedia, major media, or niche sites.

For PBNs, using drops is the standard. You restore the site or create a new niche blog on this domain, after which you place a powerful link from it to your main project (Money Site).

Key metrics when choosing a drop

To find a high-quality drop domain, you will need SEO analyzers: Ahrefs, Majestic, or Semrush. What to look out for:

  • Number of referring domains: It's not the total number of links that matters, but the number of unique domains. Preferably 50+ RD.
  • DR (Domain Rating) by Ahrefs: The higher, the better. Usually, people look for DR 20+. However, DR is easily manipulated, so always check the quality of the links themselves.
  • TF/CF (Trust Flow / Citation Flow) by Majestic: The ideal ratio of TF to CF should be around 1. If CF is much higher than TF, this is a sign of a low-quality spam profile.
  • Traffic: If the domain still has residual traffic, it's a huge plus. This means Google still trusts it.

Checking history: Wayback Machine and Google

Even a domain with a high DR can be useless or dangerous if it was previously used for black-hat schemes.

Use Web Archive (Wayback Machine) to check what was on the site before:

  • Was the domain previously used for a PBN (Chinese characters, casino, pharma)?
  • Were there any 301 redirects to other sites before the drop? (Redirects wash away the weight).
  • Does the theme of the old site match what you plan to do?

Also, check the domain's indexation in Google using the site:domain.com operator. If the domain dropped very recently and is still in the index, that's a great signal. If the domain has been free for a long time, it might have been dropped from the index, which is normal but will require effort for re-indexation.

Anchor list analysis

Be sure to review the link anchors pointing to this domain in Ahrefs. They should be natural.

Red flags in the anchor list:

  • Characters (Japanese spam).
  • Anchors from the casino niche (viagra, cialis, casino, slots).
  • Over-spamming with commercial queries (buy cheap sneakers).

The ideal anchor list consists of branded queries, URLs, and non-anchor words (here, website, read more).

Fast indexation of links from PBN

You found the perfect drop, deployed a site on it, wrote a cool article, and placed a link to your Money Site. What's next? You need Google to see it.

The problem with PBN sites is that Google crawlers do not visit them very often. You could wait for weeks for your expensive link to be indexed and start passing weight.

Conclusion

Searching for expired domains for a PBN requires patience and attention. Don't fall for pretty DR metrics without manually checking backlinks and history in Web Archive. Buy clean domains with thematic links, build high-quality content on them, and use forced indexation for quick results.
