"Discovered — Currently Not Indexed": Why Google Ignores Your Pages and How to Fix It

You open Google Search Console and see a list of pages with the status "Discovered — currently not indexed". Days pass, weeks pass — the pages are still not in Google. This is one of the most frustrating problems for website owners. We explain exactly why this happens and how to fix it once and for all.

What "Discovered — currently not indexed" Actually Means

This status means Google has found the URL — it appeared in your sitemap or someone linked to it — but Google has decided not to crawl it yet. In other words: Google knows the page exists, but chose not to visit it.

The key word here is "chosen". This is not a technical error. This is Google's scheduling decision based on your site's crawl budget and the perceived priority of that page.

💡 Quick analogy

Imagine a postman who has 1,000 letters to deliver. He knows your address exists, but if he thinks it's a low-priority delivery, he puts it at the back of the queue. Your page waits for months — while competitors' pages get indexed the same day.

The Real Reasons Google Skips Your Pages

Many SEO guides give you vague answers like "improve content quality." The truth is more specific:

  • Crawl budget exhausted. Google allocates a limited number of crawls per day per site. Large sites with thousands of pages often run out of budget — leaving new pages in the queue.
  • Low domain authority. New websites or sites with few backlinks get a smaller crawl budget. Google doesn't trust them enough to crawl aggressively.
  • Slow server response times. If your server responds slowly (over 500ms), Google's crawler slows down or stops to avoid overloading you.
  • Too many low-value pages. If your sitemap contains paginated filter pages, tag pages, or duplicate content, Google "wastes" its budget on them instead of your important content.
  • No internal linking. Pages that exist only in the sitemap but have zero internal links pointing to them are considered low-priority.
  • Recently published. New pages on established sites are sometimes in the "discovered" queue for days before being processed. For low-authority sites, this can stretch to months.

What NOT to Do (Common Mistakes)

When site owners see this status, they often make things worse:

  • Constantly requesting re-indexing through Search Console "URL Inspection" (the limit is very low — only 10-15 URLs per day manually)
  • Blocking and re-enabling pages hoping Google re-crawls them
  • Removing pages from the sitemap thinking that will help
  • Waiting indefinitely with no action — this can last for months
⚠ The manual URL inspection trap

The "Request indexing" button in Google Search Console looks like a solution, but it's severely rate-limited. You can send maybe 10-15 URLs per day this way. For a site with hundreds of affected pages, this is practically useless.

The Real Fix: Force Google to Index Through the Official API

Google provides the Indexing API specifically to allow websites to notify Google of important pages that need immediate crawling. When you send a request through the API, Google prioritizes crawling that URL — typically within 24-48 hours.

This is not a workaround or a gray-hat technique. This is Google's official recommended method for accelerating indexing.

Step 1: Set Up Google Search Console Correctly

Verify your site in Google Search Console if you haven't already. Make sure you have a well-structured sitemap submitted. Check for any manual penalties under "Manual Actions" — these can prevent indexing entirely.

Step 2: Fix Technical Issues First

Before forcing indexing, fix what's broken: ensure pages load under 3 seconds, add internal links to orphaned pages, remove genuinely low-value pages from your sitemap, and fix any crawl errors visible in Search Console.

Step 3: Use the Google Indexing API to Push Pages

This is where the real acceleration happens. Through the Indexing API you can notify Google about 200 URLs per day — Google then sends Googlebot to crawl those specific URLs with high priority. The difference vs. waiting:

❌
Without API: 2-8 weeks
✅
With API: 24-48 hours

Which Pages to Prioritize First

Don't try to index everything at once. With a 200 URL/day quota, be strategic:

  1. Revenue-generating pages first — product pages, service pages, landing pages
  2. Fresh content — new blog posts, news articles
  3. Recently updated pages — if you've significantly changed existing content
  4. Skip thin content pages — paginated results, tag pages, filter combinations

Long-Term: Prevent "Discovered" Status From Happening Again

Once you've fixed your existing backlog, set up a system so new pages never get stuck:

  • Automated daily indexing: Set IndexFast to check your sitemap every day and automatically submit new URLs. Every page you publish gets indexed that same day.
  • Internal linking: Whenever you publish new content, link to it from 2-3 existing popular pages. This signals importance to Google.
  • Sitemap hygiene: Remove pages that you don't want indexed (thin content, duplicates) from your sitemap. This gives Google's crawler more budget for your important pages.
  • Monitor Search Console: Check Coverage > "Discovered — currently not indexed" weekly. If numbers spike, investigate immediately.
